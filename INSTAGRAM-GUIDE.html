Instagram Guide

Everything you need to set up and run Kathleen's Kitchen on Instagram.

In This Guide 1. Account Setup (5 minutes) 2. Profile & Bio 3. Link in Bio 4. Posting Schedule 5. Copy-Paste Captions 6. Bake Day Shot List 7. Hashtags 8. Quick Tips

1. Account Setup

Do this with Sterling or on your own — takes about 5 minutes.

1
Create a Gmail
Go to gmail.com → "Create account" → Use something like kathleenskitchenut@gmail.com. This is just for the Instagram login — you won't use it for email.
2
Download Instagram
Get the Instagram app from the App Store (iPhone) or Google Play (Android). Open it and tap "Sign up."
3
Create the Account
Sign up with the Gmail you just made. For the username, try these in order (pick the first one available):
  • kathleenskitchen
  • kathleens_kitchen
  • kathleenskitchenut
  • kathleens.kitchen
  • kathleenskitchen_ut
4
Switch to Business Account
Go to Settings → Account → Switch to Professional Account → Choose "Business" → Category: "Bakery". This is free and unlocks the "link in bio" feature and post insights.
5
Set Profile Photo
Use the kitchen portrait from the website. It's in: images/kathleen-about.png. AirDrop it to your phone or email it to yourself.

2. Profile & Bio

Copy and paste this into your Instagram bio:

Homemade breads & sweet treats Baked fresh in Pepperwood, Utah Limited events · Order before they sell out!

Display name: Kathleen's Kitchen

The bio has a 150-character limit. The text above is exactly 99 characters — leaves room to add a seasonal line like "Easter orders open!" when events are live.

3. Link in Bio

Once the site is deployed to Netlify (see the Admin Guide), your link page will be at something like:

kathleenskitchen.netlify.app/links.html

Go to Instagram → Edit Profile → Website → paste that URL.

This page has buttons for Order Now, See Events, What We Make, and Text Kathleen — all matching the site's look.

Every post caption should end with: "Link in bio to order!" — this trains people to go to your profile to find the order link.

4. Posting Schedule

You only post around baking events. Here's the rhythm for each one:

When Type What to Post
5 days before deadline Feed post Event announcement — what's available, prices, order deadline
3 days before Story "Only X spots left!" + swipe up / link sticker
1 day before deadline Feed post "Last chance!" countdown + behind-the-scenes prep photo
Bake day 3-5 Stories Dough rising, oven shots, finished products, boxing up orders
Delivery day Feed post "All delivered!" + thank you + customer photos (with permission)
After sold out Story SOLD OUT graphic — builds excitement for next event
That's about 2-3 feed posts + 4-6 stories per event. Totally manageable even on a busy bake week.

6. Bake Day Shot List

Simple iPhone photos you can take while you bake. No fancy setup needed — natural light from a window is perfect.

1
Ingredients
Flour, butter, eggs laid out before you start
2
Dough Rising
Bowl of dough, covered with a towel
3
Shaped & Ready
Rolls on the baking sheet before the oven
4
Fresh from Oven
The money shot! Golden, steaming, beautiful
5
The Spread
Everything laid out together on the counter
6
Packaged Up
Orders boxed, bagged, and ready for pickup
Pro tip: Take photos near a window! Natural side light makes food look amazing. Avoid overhead kitchen lights — they make everything look flat and yellow.

Instagram allows up to 30 hashtags, but 8-15 focused ones perform best. The "always use" set above is your core — add 3-5 relevant ones per post.

8. Quick Tips

  • Post at 7-9am or 6-8pm — that's when Utah moms are scrolling
  • Reply to every comment — even just a heart. The algorithm rewards engagement
  • Save your best photos as Highlights — create highlight circles for "Breads," "Sweets," and "Happy Customers"
  • Repost customer photos (with permission) — nothing sells better than real people enjoying your food
  • Use the link sticker in Stories — it takes people straight to your order page
  • Don't stress about perfection — authentic, homemade vibes sell better than polished content. Your kitchen, your hands, your flour-dusted counter IS the brand
Utah Cottage Food Law reminder: You may want to check current Utah cottage food regulations for any labeling or disclosure requirements on social media sales. As of 2026, Utah allows home-baked goods sales with an annual limit — make sure you're registered if required.
